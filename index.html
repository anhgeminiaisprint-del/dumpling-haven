The Story

What Makes a Dumpling?

Almost every culinary culture across human history invented its own version of a dumpling: tender dough enveloping a savory or sweet treasure.

🥢

The Perfect Wrapper

Whether made from wheat flour, rice flour, or tapioca starch, the skin can range from paper-thin translucent crystal wrappers to hearty, chewy doughs.

🥩

The Filling

Juicy minced pork, tender chicken, earthy wild mushrooms, cabbage, melted cheeses, or potatoes spiced with aromatic ginger, garlic, and scallions.

🔥

The Technique

Pleated, crescent-pinched, purse-gathered, or fork-crimped. The fold isn't just ornamental—it locks in scalding juices and creates crisp texture.

Preparation

Three Classic Ways to Cook

Different cooking methods transform the texture of the wrapper and filling.

Soft & Delicate
♨️

Steamed (Zheng)

Cooked inside perforated bamboo steamer baskets over boiling water. Keeps wrappers supple and seals in rich meat broths like in Xiao Long Bao.

Time: 8–10 mins
Crispy & Juicy
🍳

Pan-Fried (Guotie)

Sizzled in a hot skillet with oil, then steamed with a splash of water, and finally uncovered until a golden, lacework crust forms underneath.

Time: 10–12 mins
Hearty & Smooth
🍲

Boiled (Shui Jiao)

Gently simmered directly in hot broth or water until they float buoyantly. Often served in warming chili broths or drained with a dipping sauce.

Time: 6–8 mins

Trivia & Lore

Did You Know?

A few bite-sized historical and cultural nuggets about dumplings.

Legend has it that Chinese medicinal practitioner Zhang Zhongjing invented dumplings (Jiaozi) during the Han Dynasty to treat frostbitten ears! He packed dough wraps with lamb, chili peppers, and warming herbs shaped like little ears.

Traditional Jiaozi are shaped like yuanbao (ancient Chinese silver and gold ingots). Eating them on New Year's Eve represents wrapping up wealth, prosperity, and good luck for the year ahead.

The broth is not injected! Chefs prepare a collagen-rich pork or chicken stock that cools into a firm gelatin (aspic). When diced and folded into the dumpling, the steam melts it into hot, rich soup inside the wrapper.
